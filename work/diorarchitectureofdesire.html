Project 05 · Brand strategy case

Dior and the Architecture of Desire

How can a Maison with an exceptionally rich archive keep its codes recognizable without letting heritage become repetition?

02

Archive is not the opposite of newness.

It is the material newness has to work against. Shagun Rana · independent brand strategy case
Scope

Independent brand strategy case study based on public Dior and LVMH material. No affiliation with or endorsement by Dior or LVMH is implied. Public evidence, strategic interpretation and proposed actions are deliberately separated.

Executive brief

Dior does not need more heritage. The strategic task is deciding which memory deserves to be activated, how far it can move, and when it should be left alone.

Dior has a dense vocabulary of recognizable codes: the Bar silhouette and New Look, Cannage, Dior Oblique, Lady Dior, 30 Montaigne, and recurring stories around gardens, flowers and luck. These codes did not originate at the same time and they do not perform the same role.1345

In 2025, Jonathan Anderson became Creative Director of Dior Haute Couture, Men's and Women's collections. LVMH described his debut as an act of decoding and recoding the language of the House. Dior's Spring Summer 2026 womenswear similarly describes the archive being reinterpreted and House codes refreshed.78

My strategic hypothesis: the opportunity is not to maximize the number of recognizable Dior codes in every expression. It is to steward them as a portfolio, protecting some, stretching others, and allowing seasonal creativity to create tension around them.

02 · The current creative context

The public language around the Anderson era is already about recoding, not preservation.

LVMH described Jonathan Anderson's first Dior menswear show in June 2025 as decoding the House language in order to recode it. That collection brought the Bar jacket into Donegal tweed, recontextualized archive dresses, transformed the Book Tote with literary covers, and included a Lady Dior reinterpretation by Sheila Hicks.7

Dior's Spring Summer 2026 womenswear describes heritage being approached with empathy and wit, with archive pieces reinterpreted and House codes refreshed. Anderson's first Dior haute couture collection in January 2026 treated couture as living knowledge, with nature, craft and creative continuity at its centre.89

By Autumn Winter 2026/2027, that recoding logic was still visible: LVMH described revisited Bar jackets and garden references in the Tuileries womenswear show, while the July 2026 couture collection translated Lynda Benglis into sculptural Dior craft.1112

Observed Bar in Donegal tweed

A foundational silhouette moved through a materially unexpected lens.

Observed Archive dresses recontextualized

Historical forms were brought into a new menswear conversation rather than simply reproduced.

Observed Lady Dior as a creative platform

The icon continues to absorb new artistic and material interpretations.

03 · Strategic tension

Too fixed

Heritage becomes quotation.

If a code is repeated without transformation, recognition can remain high while creative energy falls.

THE USEFUL MIDDLE Memory gives the client something to recognize. Mutation gives them a reason to look again.
Too loose

Novelty loses lineage.

If creative change breaks too many links at once, the work may be interesting but less legibly connected to the House.

These are strategic risks to monitor, not claims that Dior currently suffers from either condition.

04 · Strategic proposal

A Code Stewardship System for deciding what stays, what stretches and what changes completely.

Layer 01 House constants

Ideas whose value comes from continuity: couture construction, the architectural relationship to the body, the origin story of 30 Montaigne, and the House's commitment to exceptional craft.

Protect the principle, not one frozen expression.
Layer 02 Elastic codes

Recognizable assets that can migrate in material, scale and category: Cannage, Dior Oblique, floral and lucky motifs, and selected icon signatures.

Stretch them, but keep the lineage intelligible.
Layer 03 Icon platforms

Objects such as Lady Dior that can hold recurring artistic reinterpretation while retaining an identifiable core form and story.

Use repetition to build memory and mutation to renew desire.
Layer 04 Seasonal disruption

Proportion, casting, styling, cultural reference, collaboration and unexpected material choices can change aggressively because not every element needs to carry heritage at once.

This is where surprise earns its space.

05 · The working tool

Build a House Code Ledger, not a moodboard.

A useful code library should record more than images. It should define what makes a code itself, what can change, what should remain legible, and where the code is already heavily activated.

Field What it records Why it matters
Origin

The documented historical source, date and original context.

Prevents vague heritage storytelling and incorrect attribution.

Essential property

The part that makes the code recognizable: geometry, silhouette, construction, object form, place or story.

Separates identity from decoration.

Mutable properties

Material, colour, scale, proportion, placement, category and cultural lens that may change.

Creates permission for designers to move without losing the root.

Current activation

Where the code is appearing now across fashion, leather goods, beauty, home, content and retail.

Makes overuse and underuse visible across teams.

Client meaning

What the code helps a client recognize, feel or understand about Dior.

Keeps heritage connected to desire rather than internal history alone.

06 · The pressure test

Before a heritage code enters a major launch, ask five questions.

01Can the Dior lineage be explained precisely?

If the origin cannot be stated accurately, the reference is too vague to carry strategic meaning.

02What is actually changing?

A new colour alone is not always a new interpretation. Material, function, scale, proportion or context should create a real point of view.

03Would it still be interesting without the logo?

The strongest House memory should also live in silhouette, craft, material or object architecture.

04Does the category transform the code?

Moving Cannage from leather to crystal, fragrance or textile should exploit what the new medium can do rather than copy a surface mechanically.

05Is this the right moment to activate it?

A strong code can lose power through saturation. Dormancy can preserve future surprise.

07 · From design language to client meaning

The code should travel further than the runway.

Product Design with lineage

Each major icon update should know which element carries memory, which element creates mutation, and which craft detail proves the transformation.

Campaign Show the tension

Creative communication should reveal the relationship between archive and present rather than relying on heritage language as a generic backdrop.

Retail Make history usable

Client advisors need concise code stories that explain why a product is recognizably Dior and exactly what is new about the current expression.

Digital Build a lineage layer

For selected icons, product storytelling can surface three things consistently: origin, current mutation and craft proof.

08 · Proposed pilot

Test the system on one code before turning it into governance.

I would start with Cannage because Dior publicly uses it as a House signature and it already moves across leather goods, fashion, home and beauty related objects. That makes it a useful test of whether a code can remain recognizable while the medium changes.23510

Weeks 1 to 2 Audit

Map current public Cannage expressions across categories. Record material, scale, role, story and level of visual prominence.

Weeks 3 to 4 Define

Write the ledger entry: historical origin, essential geometry, mutable properties, boundaries and current activation.

Weeks 5 to 8 Prototype

Develop product, editorial, retail and digital applications that each interpret the code differently while preserving lineage.

Weeks 9 to 12 Test

Evaluate recognition, understanding, perceived newness, advisor usability and whether the code remains legible without repeated logo reinforcement.

09 · What I would measure

Recognition is only one part of successful renewal.

RecognitionUnaided Dior association

Can clients identify the House from selected visual or material cues without the logo being shown?

LineageCorrect code recall

After the experience, can clients accurately connect the current expression to its Dior origin?

RenewalPerceived newness

Does the expression feel current and creatively distinct rather than archival reproduction?

ClientelingAdvisor story usability

Can advisors explain the origin and mutation naturally in a client conversation without sounding scripted?

ExplorationCross category discovery

Does learning a code in one category increase interest in seeing how Dior expresses it elsewhere?

DisciplineCode concentration

How heavily is each code being activated across a season, market and category before saturation becomes a concern?

10 · Guardrails

Stewardship should increase creative freedom, not turn the archive into a rulebook.

Do not score creativity into submission.

The ledger is a shared memory tool and decision aid. It should not become a numerical approval system that rewards safe repetition.

Do not make every code omnipresent.

Selective absence is part of preserving recognition and surprise.

Do not flatten categories.

A fashion code translated into beauty, jewelry or home should respect the material logic and client expectations of that category.

Do not confuse heritage with explanation.

The client does not need a history lesson at every touchpoint. The story should appear where it makes the object more meaningful.

Do not invent provenance.

If an origin or historical connection cannot be verified, it should not be presented as House heritage.

Recommendation

Dior's archive is most powerful when it behaves like a grammar: recognizable enough to create continuity, flexible enough to produce sentences nobody has seen before.

The Anderson era already demonstrates active recoding in public. My recommendation is therefore not a new creative direction for Dior. It is an operating layer that could help product, communication, retail and client teams describe and steward that creative logic consistently.

The ambition is simple: protect memory without protecting sameness.
