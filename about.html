About

There was never a clean pivot. Just a pattern I noticed late.

I kept moving toward the same question in different clothes: what makes people care enough to remember, choose or act?

I like beautiful things. I like understanding them even more.

01

I started by writing in public.

I began this website during lockdown at fifteen because I had too many questions and nowhere sensible to put them. I wrote about books, films, culture, social issues and whatever else had taken over my brain that week. The archive is messy. I am keeping it that way.

Writing taught me to notice patterns in what people pay attention to, what they remember and what they choose to care about.

02

Then I tried to make people care.

Through Girl Up, menstrual health projects, Womensture and other early social impact work, I learned something that still shapes how I think: a good idea is useless if nobody understands it, trusts it or acts on it.

The subject was different from luxury. The underlying problem was surprisingly similar. Attention. Meaning. Trust. Action.

03

Computer science taught me systems.

I studied computer science. It trained me to break complicated things into parts, look for structure, and ask what changes when one decision changes the whole system. I do not want to spend my career writing software, but I do not want to erase that training either.

It is part of how I think now: creatively, but with an instinct for structure.

04

Luxury made the pattern obvious.

Luxury brought together the subjects I kept circling anyway: psychology, culture, storytelling, aesthetics, client experience and commerce. It is one of the few industries where a material, a room, a sentence, a ritual or a pause can all carry strategic meaning.

That level of detail is what pulled me in.

05

The long game is ownership.

Right now, I am building toward luxury brand strategy and marketing. I want to learn from people operating at the highest level, get very good at the craft, and understand how strong brands create both cultural meaning and commercial value.

Eventually, I want to build companies of my own. I want the craft before the title and the evidence before the confidence.

Away from PowerPoint

My taste did not begin with a trend forecast.

I loved Emily in Paris, but Paris was already in my head through Funny Face and That Girl. I keep returning to old fashion magazines, perfume counters, films with impossible wardrobes, beautiful packaging and people who are obsessively good at unusual things.

I am ambitious. Probably excessively so. Underneath that, I mostly like learning how things work, why people care, and how both could be made better.
