Why We Want What We Want

Objects are rarely just objects once people begin attaching stories to them.

Essay by Shagun Rana · 2026

The interesting part is not the purchase.

I am fascinated by the moment before it. The tiny sequence of associations that turns something ordinary into something emotionally charged. A bag can become proof that you made it. A perfume can become a person you miss. A lipstick can become a version of yourself you are trying on before anyone else sees her.

That is why branding interests me. Not because logos are beautiful, but because meaning can accumulate around an object until the object begins to carry much more than its material value.

Desire is social, but it is also intimate.

Some purchases are performed for other people. Others are private rituals. Most are both. Status, memory, taste, belonging, aspiration and identity overlap in ways that make simple explanations feel incomplete.

The best brands do not merely tell us what a product is. They help us decide what owning it might mean.

The question I want to keep asking

When does a brand create genuine emotional value, and when does it simply manufacture insecurity? I do not think the answer is clean. That messiness is exactly what makes the subject worth studying.
